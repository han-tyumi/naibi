## Why these two, and what the pairing was for

Both are banked games whose entries are mostly *rates* — house edges, payout
odds, commissions — and the adding-games handoff already records the failure mode
those invite: a house edge quoted against a different denominator from the
source's, which makes our number read as a contradiction of the first page a
reader would check. That is what this sitting went looking for.

**It found one wrong payout and one inverted prevalence claim, and the rest of
the arithmetic held.** Baccarat's entire drawing tableau is exact, rank by rank —
Banker stands on 7; draws on 6 against a 6 or 7; on 5 against 4 to 7; on 4
against 2 to 7; on 3 against everything but an 8; always on 0, 1 or 2 — and so
are 1.06 percent for Banker, 1.24 for Player and 14.4 for the tie at 8 to 1. Red
Dog's whole payout ladder is exact too: 5 to 1 at a spread of one, 4 to 1 at two,
2 to 1 at three, even money from four up, 11 to 1 for a third card matching a
pair, pushes on adjacent ends and on a pair that does not triple, and the raise
threshold of seven that is the game's only decision.

## `baccarat` — five

1. **A payout was wrong.** The pair side bet was given at **10 to 1**. One source
   states it twice, once for each hand: Player Pair and Banker Pair both pay
   **11 to 1**, and the house keeps about 11.3 percent. This is the finding the
   sitting was looking for and the only outright wrong number in either entry.
   The same source lists an Either Pair bet at 5 to 1 for about 14.5 percent,
   which the entry did not carry and now does.
2. **A prevalence claim ran backwards.** "A few tables, mostly British, pay 9 to
   1" on the tie. The source says **most** casinos in the United Kingdom do, and
   gives the resulting edge as about 4.85 percent. The entry understated a
   majority as a handful, and dropped the figure that makes the point.
3. **A ratio was loose against its own denominators.** The tie was called "some
   twelve times the price of either alternative". Against Player at 1.24 that is
   about 11.6; against Banker at 1.06 it is about 13.6. "Twelve times either" is
   wrong in both directions at once; it now says better than eleven times, which
   is true of both.
4. **The cut card was placed flatly where the sources disagree.** "Buries a cut
   card sixteen from the bottom." One source says sixteen cards from the bottom,
   the other says in front of the seventh from last. Both are now given.
5. **"Nothing further is available to a player"** was stated absolutely. Card
   counting really is a rounding error — one source puts it at about 0.05 percent
   — but the same sentence goes on to say that counting paired with edge sorting
   can give a significant edge over the casino. The entry now names it, and says
   what it is: exploiting the equipment rather than playing the game.

**A documented variant was missing.** Super 6, also sold as Punto 2000, drops the
commission entirely and instead pays a winning Banker bet only half when the
Banker wins with exactly 6. The source gives the arithmetic: the Banker edge
rises from about 1.06 to about 1.46 percent, equivalent to charging nearly 5.9
percent rather than 5, and the Banker wins with a 6 about five times in an
eight-deck shoe. Added.

**The entry's own best line survived.** "The table looks arbitrary because it is
a fossil" — that the tableau records the choices a good player would once have
made when both two-card hands were hidden — is the kind of explanation this audit
usually has to delete. It is not contradicted by either source, and the sources
independently confirm the mechanical fact it rests on: in punto banco every move
is forced, while chemin de fer and banque leave the choices to people.

## `red-dog` — three

1. **The house edge was rounded loosely and the entry disagreed with itself.**
   `setup` said the bank's share falls "from around 3.2 percent on a single deck
   to around 2.8 percent on **six or more**"; `goal_and_scoring` said the same
   range but "at **eight**". The source gives 3.155 percent at one deck and 2.751
   at eight, and says nothing about six. Both places now say about 3.16 and about
   2.75, and both say eight.
2. **The pot version was described as unbanked, and the fuller source's usual
   form is banked.** The In-Between variant opened "Nobody banks: everyone antes
   into a pot". The source gives two formats and calls the *dealer as banker* the
   most usual one — the dealer alone stakes the pot and plays against each of the
   others in turn — with the everybody-antes arrangement as the alternative. The
   variant now gives both.
3. **Two rules of that version were missing**, both from the other source:
   posting on an ace costs four times the bet rather than double, and a pair for
   the two end cards is played as a higher-or-lower guess, with a third card of
   the same rank costing treble.

## Recorded, not acted on

- **`red-dog`'s High Card Pool variant rests on nothing read.** One source names
  High Card Pool and Shoot as alternative names for the older, different game
  that also answers to Red Dog — which is what the entry's own background says,
  correctly — but neither source gives that game's rules. The five-card wager,
  the ante-to-pot structure and the claim that Slippery Sam and Shoot are the
  same thing with three cards and a bank are unsupported here. Slippery Sam is
  not named by either source at all.
- **`red-dog`'s alias "Scottish foghorn" is missing**, and one source has it.
  Left for the alias sweep, like the unconfirmed names in earlier records.
- **The two sources classify the family differently.** One treats Acey Deucey and
  Yablon as alternative names for In Between, with Red Dog as the casino
  version's name; the other treats acey deucey as "a close variant" of red dog
  rather than a name for it. The entry lists all three as aliases, which follows
  the first. Named here rather than changed.
- **`baccarat`'s reduced-commission figures are in neither source** — four
  percent, 2.75 percent, the Las Vegas casino that charged nothing in 1989, and
  the London rule waiving commission on a Banker two-card three beating a Player
  who drew an 8, taking the edge to about 0.81 percent. The variant is left with
  its hedges.
- **`red-dog`'s casino shuffling practice** — a cut card buried in the shoe, the
  round it appears in being the last — is in neither of that entry's sources,
  though it is in `baccarat`'s. Left alone.

## The originality pass, run after the fact fixes

**Both entries clean**, with no finding at any tier.

**Both tools controlled in both directions**, and one control had to be run
twice. The first attempt planted a sentence in `red-dog` built around the source
phrase "A close variant is acey deucey" — six words, one short of the seven-word
bar, so it was never going to be reported. The run came back showing only the
pagat plant, which would have read as "the Wikipedia file is not live" if taken
at face value. It was re-run with a twenty-word sentence from the same file,
which came back correctly attributed. **The fault was in the plant, not the
tool** — the second time in two sittings that the first reading of a control was
wrong, and the reason the rule is to read the output rather than the exit code.

The other three plants landed first time: 23 words off pagat and 19 off Wikipedia
for `baccarat`, 24 off pagat for `red-dog`.

**One claim introduced by this sitting's own correction was caught before the
stamp**, which is the ninth sitting running. The sentence added about edge
sorting called it "the one technique reported to turn a real profit", where the
source says counting *paired with* edge sorting can give a significant edge — a
stronger and narrower claim than the source makes, written into a sentence whose
purpose was to soften an absolute one.

**A note on the sources themselves.** Wikipedia's plain-text extract drops
tables, and both of these articles keep numbers in them: the red dog article's
spread, house-edge and probability tables all came through as bare headings with
nothing under them. Everything the entry says about the payout ladder was checked
against the other source, which sets the same figures out in prose. **A source
that answers 200 and reads back its own title can still be missing exactly the
part you came for.**
